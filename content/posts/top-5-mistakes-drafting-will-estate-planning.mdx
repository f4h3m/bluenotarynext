Drafting a last will and testament is one of the most critical steps in safeguarding your family's future and ensuring your assets are distributed according to your wishes. Unfortunately, minor drafting errors or procedural oversights can result in lengthy probate battles, expensive legal fees, or even the complete invalidation of your testamentary documents.

## Mistake 1: Failing to Update Designated Beneficiaries

A will does not automatically override beneficiary designations on retirement accounts (401k, IRA), life insurance policies, or payable-on-death (POD) bank accounts. If you name an ex-spouse as a beneficiary on your policy 10 years ago, that institution will pay out directly to them regardless of what your new will states.

- Review and align all non-probate asset beneficiary designations annually.
- Coordinate primary and contingent beneficiary elections with your will clauses.

## Mistake 2: Overlooking Digital Assets and Accounts

Modern estates include substantial digital footprints—cryptocurrency wallets, cloud storage, social media accounts, and online business revenue streams.

- Include explicit language granting your executor the legal authority to access and manage digital accounts under the Revised Uniform Fiduciary Access to Digital Assets Act (RUFADAA).
- Store master passwords or key recovery instructions in an encrypted digital safe.

## Mistake 3: Selecting a Single Executor with No Successors

Appointing only one executor leaves your estate vulnerable if that individual predeceases you, falls ill, or declines to serve.

- Always name at least one or two alternate successor executors.
- Confirm ahead of time that your chosen personal representatives are willing and capable of handling administrative duties.

## Mistake 4: Not Establishing Contingency Clauses for Beneficiaries

Failing to state what happens if a named beneficiary predeceases you can cause assets to lapse back into residue or trigger intestacy rules. Clearly indicate whether bequests are distributed *per stirpes* (to the beneficiary's descendants) or *per capita*.

## Mistake 5: Improper Witnessing and Notarization

State probate courts enforce strict execution formalities. A will signed without the required number of disinterested witnesses or lacking a statutory self-proving affidavit can cause intense scrutiny during probate.

- Use remote online notarization with recorded video verification to establish capacity and eliminate forgery allegations.
- Ensure all witnesses are independent and not named as beneficiaries in the will.
